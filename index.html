Abstract

Open-vocabulary 3D scene segmentation aims to segment 3D scenes given arbitrary text-specified queries. Existing feature-distillation methods project 2D vision-language features into 3D to train a point network, but are costly and fragile due to imperfect indoor teachers and distillation degradation. In this paper, we propose SAM3D-OV, a training-free pipeline for open-vocabulary 3D semantic segmentation and text-queryable 3D mask extraction. SAM3D-OV first queries SAM3 on posed RGB-D frames to obtain language-aware 2D masks and lifts them into 3D through depth-consistent projection. SAM3D-OV consolidates these observations in two stages. First, a superpoint graph aggregates and propagates semantic evidence over geometry- and color-consistent neighborhoods, converting sparse view-dependent observations into a denser 3D semantic field. Second, an instance-aware completion step treats each reliable projected mask as a seed and expands it to nearby compatible superpoints, preserving the grouping information provided by the 2D mask. Across ScanNet and Replica, SAM3D-OV consistently improves over direct multi-view mask voting and compares favorably with representative open-vocabulary 3D baselines, without any 3D network training. These results suggest that modern 2D open-vocabulary segmentation models, when paired with explicit 3D consolidation, offer a simple and reproducible alternative to heavyweight 3D feature distillation.

Method

Given a colored point cloud, posed RGB-D frames, and text prompts, SAM3D-OV assigns each point a prompt label or an unknown label. The vocabulary can be changed at inference. The pipeline uses pretrained SAM3 predictions without training a 3D network on the target dataset.

01 / MASK LIFTING

Project language-aware observations

Query SAM3 on each selected RGB frame. A 3D point receives mask evidence only when it projects inside the mask and agrees with the observed depth. Confidence-weighted observations are accumulated across views.

02 / GRAPH CONSOLIDATION

Share evidence across coherent regions

Group points into superpoints and connect nearby regions. Semantic scores propagate along geometry and color edges while remaining anchored to the original mask evidence, producing a denser 3D semantic field.

03 / INSTANCE COMPLETION

Grow compatible object regions

Treat reliable projected masks as seeds. Expand each seed into connected superpoints that support the same label, preserving the grouping cue of the original 2D mask. Resolve overlapping regions using graph evidence.

Implementation

The experiments use a 0.05 m depth-consistency threshold and 0.05 m spatial voxels. Semantic consolidation runs for 12 iterations with propagation weight 0.55. The color-aware variant splits each voxel into four coarse RGB bins. Runtime is dominated by SAM3 inference over the selected frames and text prompts.

Qualitative examples

Point-level segmentation on ScanNet and Replica. Columns compare ground truth, ConceptFusion+CLIP, SAM3 weighted voting, and SAM3D-OV.

Direct SAM3 voting already captures many major surfaces and objects, but neighboring points can receive fragmented or conflicting assignments. Graph consolidation shares evidence across coherent regions, while instance completion uses individual mask hypotheses to recover more complete object-like regions.

Results

We evaluate point-level semantic segmentation on ScanNet20 and the reduced Replica label set. All-point mIoU includes uncovered points. Valid-only mIoU measures predictions only where a label is assigned. Coverage is the fraction of points assigned a non-unknown label.

SAM3D-OV reaches 48.3 scene-average all-point mIoU, improving on OpenScene-LSeg fused at 38.3 by 10.0 points. Direct SAM3 weighted voting is already strong on Replica, reaching 44.6 all-point mIoU and 48.8 valid-only mIoU. SAM3D-OV raises these to 49.0 and 53.6, gains of 4.4 and 4.8 points. Coverage also increases from 95.8% to 98.6%, showing that consolidation can recover additional regions even when initial mask lifting is relatively dense.

The aggregation metric matters here. Dataset-level mIoU decreases from 51.7 for weighted voting to 50.7 for SAM3D-OV, despite the improvement in scene-average scores. Pooling all points changes the weighting of scenes and classes. SAM3D-OV improves the average scene result, but does not improve every aggregate metric on Replica.

Geometry-only semantic propagation increases coverage from 74.8% to 94.4% and all-point mIoU from 34.6 to 43.0. This 8.4-point gain indicates that local 3D structure can recover points missed by partial 2D masks. Instance-aware completion then raises all-point mIoU to 46.0 and valid-only mIoU to 47.6, supporting the value of preserving mask-level grouping beyond semantic smoothing.

The final color-aware completion reaches 46.1 all-point mIoU and 57.7 dataset-level mIoU, compared with 34.6 and 50.5 for direct voting. Its scene-average gain over geometry-only completion is small at 0.1 points. Color-aware semantic propagation alone also slightly trails geometry-only propagation. Most of the improvement comes from graph propagation and instance completion, with color grouping giving a modest additional benefit in the final configuration.

Limitations

SAM3D-OV depends on the quality of its 2D observations. Geometry can improve completeness but cannot recover semantic evidence when SAM3 consistently misses or mislabels an object. The method also requires posed RGB-D observations and a reconstructed point cloud, making it best suited to scene-level processing with reliable camera calibration and depth.

Runtime grows with the number of selected frames and text prompts. The paper identifies cached mask observations and adaptive view selection as potential ways to reduce this cost.
